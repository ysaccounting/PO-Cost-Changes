            1. What the Engine Does

            The Engine brings every card and bank transaction for each of your companies into one place, works out what each one is, and posts it to that company's QuickBooks Online file. For each transaction it can:

            • Import it automatically from a card program (Slash, Divvy, Taekus, Global Rewards), from a bank or card connected through Plaid, or from a file you upload.
            • Match it to an open bill in QuickBooks and pay that bill, scoring how confident the match is.
            • Suggest a vendor and category from your Bank Feed Rules, from card-payment and season ticket holder (STH) logic, or from how people have coded the same merchant before.
            • Check season ticket holder charges against the STH records kept in Airtable: that a record exists, that its status is Active, and that the charge falls on the expected day for the expected amount.
            • Post the result to QuickBooks: a bill payment, an expense, or a transfer, and record every step in the Audit Log.

            Nothing reaches QuickBooks unless a person posts it, or a setting or rule you have switched on says to post it automatically.

            Sign in with your username and password, then the two-step code. Working steadily keeps you signed in; after a period of inactivity you are signed out and asked to sign in again.

            Common controls

            • Quick search at the top of a grid filters it as you type; on the Bank Feed it searches once you pause typing (or press Enter).
            • Column headers sort the grid; drag a header's edge to resize a column. Each grid, and each tab of a screen, remembers its own widths; before any are dragged, every column is wide enough for its header on one line. The Excel icon exports the grid; it turns while the file is being made, and a message at the top says so.
            • Every screen opens on its first tab when chosen from the navigation, whichever tab it was left on.
            • Hover menus: rest the pointer on a navigation button (Bank Feed, QBO Sync, Broker Data, Configuration, Teams & Payment Plans, Algorithms, Tags) and its tabs are listed; choose one to open the screen straight on it.
            • Long dropdowns type to narrow in the Broker Data windows (files, tabs, bases, tables and columns): click one and type part of a name, then choose with the mouse or the arrow keys and Enter.
            • Green buttons add or save, red removes or deletes, blue closes or continues. In the navigation and tab rows, the screen and tab you are on are solid blue; light blue shows what is under the pointer.
            • Pop-up window titles capitalize every word except small ones (and, the, of ...). In a confirmation the red choice (Delete, Reset) is on the left and the other on the right.
            • A row's Save is always shown: gray while there is nothing to save, green once the row is changed. A row's Clear (red outline) empties the new row.
            • Every pop-up window closes with the red X in its top-right corner, which also means "no" on a confirmation.
            • A red x on a grid row (white, with a red outline) asks first: removing a row -- a rule, a team, a source, a key -- is confirmed in a pop-up before anything changes. The x's that clear a search or a filter, and the lines of a split being built, do not ask.
            • Signing out happens after 60 minutes with no activity.
            • Undo (the red-outlined icon in the Bank Feed grid's toolbar, shown when there is something to undo) reverses your last action. Hover over it to see what it will undo.

            What Undo can and can't reverse

            Undo reverses your own most recent action -- one step, not a history -- in the company it was taken, QuickBooks included: a posting it made is removed or put back as it was.

            Undo reversesUndo can't reverse
            Add (posting a transaction), one row or Add All -- the posting is removed from QuickBooksDelete on Categorized (post the transaction again from For Review)
            Match, one row or Match All -- the payment is removed from QuickBooksSplits, Force Match and Match Transfer
            ExcludeDelete Permanently (Excluded), Delete Bill and Split Bill (Open Bills)
            An edit to a For Review transaction's vendor, category, QBO memo, profile, bank detail or custom noteAn edit to a transaction already in QuickBooks (edit it back)
            An Open Bills change to a bill's vendor or amountConvert Bill Payments to Expenses
            Rules, profiles, teams, payment plans, suggestions, settings, uploads, syncs, pulls, and anything done by a scheduled job

            Something Undo can't reverse is changed back where it was made: Return to For Review or Delete for a posting, the grid or window it came from for everything else.

            3. The Bank Feed

            Tabs

            TabWhat is in it
            For ReviewNew transactions waiting for a decision. The count beside the tab is how many.
            CategorizedTransactions that have been decided and posted to QuickBooks.
            ExcludedTransactions set aside, such as duplicates or anything that should never be posted. They can be restored with Unexclude or removed with Delete Permanently.
            Open BillsThe company's unpaid bills in QuickBooks, the other side of each match. The line above the quick search says how many are mirrored and when they were last read (hourly); Sync From QBO reads them now. Its columns include Memo and Class (the Class on the bill's lines); Filters narrow by Class and Aging, among others.
            All BillsEvery bill in QuickBooks, open or paid, in the same grid. It starts filtered to Current bills (Aging); clear the filter for the rest. Its line says how many are mirrored; bills changed in QuickBooks are read hourly, and Sync From QBO reads them all again. Only open bills are ever offered as matches.

            Three more buttons sit at the end of the same row. Bank Feed Rules opens the rules that set vendor and category; Suggestions lists the vendor and category this company's past coding suggests (below); and Credit Card Profiles opens the rules that set the Profile for general cards. All three keep the Bank Feed's tabs in view; clicking any tab returns to the charges.

            Filters

            • Companies (Multi-Company only) narrows the view to chosen companies.
            • Card Accounts narrows to chosen cards and accounts. With nothing ticked, every card is shown. Cards are grouped into Virtuals and Non-Virtuals, and can be picked by how they arrive: API, Plaid or None.
            • Transaction Types narrows to charges, refunds or payments.
            • CC Last 4/5 is a card's last four digits -- five for an American Express card: from the bank detail or memo, or else its parent card's account digit before the last four (read from a five-digit number in the card account's name, or its Plaid connection).
            • Filters opens the full set: dates, amounts, vendor, category, profile, memo, match strength, STH record, and more. Strengths / Records: Record Found lists charges with an STH record; Record Not Found lists those showing No Records Found; with nothing ticked, the charges with neither a bill nor an STH pill. Charges never looked up are looked up in the background as they arrive and after each STH sync, so the lists are complete. Nothing changes until you press Apply; closing the window without it leaves the filters as they were. The button turns blue while any of these is on.

            Every picker starts with nothing ticked, which shows everything: tick only the ones you want. Clear goes back to everything. The grid reloads once, when a picker closes, rather than on every tick.

            Custom Notes

            The Custom Notes column, after QBO Memo, holds your own note on a transaction: click its pencil, type, and press Enter. A note is added to the QuickBooks memo -- and to each line's description -- in brackets, after the text and before {Engine} (a posted transaction's memo is updated when the note changes). Notes can be sorted, found with the quick search, and exported; Update Custom Notes in Batch Actions sets one note on every ticked row.

            Selecting rows

            Tick rows to act on them together. Beneath the grid, the selection is totaled separately for charges, refunds and payments. When a whole page is ticked and there are more matching rows, a blue Select all N matching link selects every one across all pages; once it is used, the same place offers Deselect all. On every grid, ticks are cleared when you move to another tab, screen or company, and with a quick search narrowing the grid, the select-all box ticks only the rows showing.

            Force MatchFor Review. Choose any open bill and pay it, outside the usual date and amount limits.

            Split BillOpen Bills. Divides a bill into parts, so each part can be paid by a different charge.

            Batch Actions

            With rows ticked, Batch Actions offers the actions that apply to the current tab:

            Batch actionWhat it does
            Add AllPosts every ticked row with its vendor and category. Rows missing either are skipped and listed.
            Match AllPays each ticked row's top bill match. Rows without a match are skipped.
            Update FieldsOne window with a column each for Vendor, Category and Custom Notes. Whatever is filled in goes on every ticked row; a field left blank keeps each row's own. Update & Add (For Review) also adds them. On Categorized a bill payment keeps its bill's vendor and category.
            Split TransactionsDivides each ticked row across several categories by percentage.
            ExcludeMoves the ticked rows to Excluded.

            On Open Bills, a bill's amount (its pencil, or the Amount column of Update Fields for several) can be: a new amount; increased or decreased by a fixed amount or a percentage (as a change to the amount or an Other Fees line); Adjust for CAD to USD -- the amount times the CAD rate on the bill's date ($100 becomes $73.51), always as a change to the amount; or Adjust for Vendors Open -- a negative "Due from Vendors - Open" line for the open balance, so it comes to zero. An Other Fees or Vendors Open line carries the first line's memo. The window shows the bill's number, vendor, date, amount and memo.

            Match Strength

            Every transaction with a candidate bill gets a score from 0 to 100% and a band. The % pill beside each candidate and the Match Strength column use the same colors:

            BandStarts atColor
            Perfect100%Deep green
            Very Strong95%Green
            Strong84%Light green
            Moderate50%Amber
            WeakBelow 50%Red

            These are the starting bands. In Algorithms > Strengths on the Dashboard you can rename bands, change where each starts and its color, add new bands and remove them. The pills, the Strengths filter and the automatic categorizing choices all follow. The lowest band always starts at 0%.

            Season ticket holder (STH) charges

            When a charge's bank details name a team, the Engine looks up the holder's STH record (read from the broker's Airtable, Google Sheets or OneDrive; see Broker Data in section 6) and checks four things: the record exists, its status is Active for the designated renewal year(s), the charge date falls on the plan's charge day, and the amount matches the expected installment. For Canadian teams the expected amount is converted from CAD at the rate for the charge's own date, shown as a third factor in the working -- unless the broker's Season Ticket Records say their costs are already in USD. The expected amount is the season total times the plan's percentage for the charge's month (or the total over the plan's months); hovering over Exp Amt shows the working. The total is one season's: where records are kept per season (a row or a tab per season), the charge's own season is used, or the newest chosen renewal year that has records. Each renewal year's pill comes from that year's own record.

            How the expected charge amount is worked out

            Exp Amt on the STH line is what this installment should be. Hovering over it shows the working. It is built in four steps:

            1. Season total. The holder's seat rows for this team, added up, for one season: the charge's own season (by its date and the team's Start Month: from July, 2026 charges pay into 26/27), or, when the records carry no status for that season, the newest chosen renewal year that has records. When some rows are renewed (by the broker's Renewal Status Words), only those are added.
            2. This month's share. The total times the plan's percentage for the charge's month, from Teams & Payment Plans. A first charge that lands the month before the plan starts takes the first month's share. A team with no percentages splits the total evenly over the plan's months.
            3. CAD to USD. For a Canadian team whose costs are in Canadian dollars, the amount is then multiplied by the CAD to USD rate for the charge's own date (Tables > CAD/USD). This is skipped when the broker's Season Ticket Records say their costs are already in USD (Costs Are In).
            4. The check. The charge passes (green check) when it is within the tolerance set in Algorithms > Monthly Payments of that figure; the variance shows as a signed percentage.
            ExampleWorking
            US team, plan charges 10% in October$3,270.00 × 10% (October) = $327.00
            Even split over 10 months$3,270.00 ÷ 10 months = $327.00
            Canadian team, costs in CADTotal cost CA$5,000.00 × 10% (October) × 0.7351 (CAD → USD on 10/1/2026) = $367.55

            A refund is never compared with the plan: it shows the season account, but no expected amount, day or variance.

            The Profile column

            The Profile column is the person a charge belongs to. It comes from the cardholder name, or from Credit Card Profiles for general cards used for several people; a profile set by a Credit Card Profiles rule has the blue RULE badge after it, and hovering over it names the rule.

            Typing a profile in for a charge also fills every other For Review charge on the same card (same card account and last four) whose profile is still blank.

            Credit Card Profiles

            The button at the end of the Bank Feed's tab row. Rules that set the Profile for general cards: card account -- one or several, ticked in the same picker as the Bank Feed's, from every card account in the company's QuickBooks file, connected or not -- bank detail (Contains or Any), team, and the profile. Tick rows (or every row in the header) for Batch Actions, as on Bank Feed Rules: set the Card Account, Bank Detail Rule, Team or Profile of every ticked row, or delete them. The first matching row wins; drag a row by its handle at the left to change the order (saved at once), and its red x removes it at once. A new row is filled in on the grid's last row and added with its Add button, as on Bank Feed Rules; Download Template and Upload From Excel sit after the quick search. A changed row shows its own Save; saving also fills the profiles of charges already in For Review. A profile a rule filled in follows the rule: when the rule is changed or removed, the charge takes the new rule's profile or gets its own name back (a profile someone typed in since is left alone). Profiles are per company, so the button is not shown in Multi-Company.

            Uploading transactions

            1. Press Upload and choose the card or bank account the file belongs to -- only accounts in use, and active in QuickBooks, are offered.
            2. Optionally enter a start date, an end date or both -- only transactions from the start and up to the end are taken; with neither, the whole file is -- then choose the file.
            3. The Engine recognizes the exports of Divvy, EvoPay, Flex, Global Rewards, Plaid, Slash, Taekus and WEX, and the pre-formatted export. Any other CSV is read by its column names: a date, a description, and either an Amount column or separate Debit and Credit columns. A few lines above the header row are skipped.
            4. If every amount in the file has the same sign (all positive or all negative), you are asked whether they are Money In or Money Out. This is asked every time.

            Uploading the same file twice is safe: transactions already loaded are skipped and reported. A card program's own transaction id is matched across every card account; a file without one is matched within the card account it is uploaded to, so the same rows uploaded to another card are that card's.

            Bank Feed Rules

            The Bank Feed Rules button opens the rules that set vendor and category. A rule has conditions (bank detail contains or equals a value, card account, amount), the vendor and category to set (or a split), and a mode:

            ModeEffect
            FillFills in Vendor and Category on For Review. A person still posts it.
            Fill & AddFills them in and posts to QuickBooks automatically.
            OffDoes nothing; the rule stays in the list.

            Inside a company, All Rules narrows the grid to Shared or Individual rules (Select All, Clear and Close, as on the Bank Feed's filters). Rules are checked top to bottom, and the first match wins, so a narrow rule placed above a broad one beats it. The # column numbers the rules in the order they are tried. Drag a rule by its handle at the left to change the order; it is saved at once (inside a company its own rules move; in Multi-Company, the shared ones). Download Template and Upload Rules From Excel load many rules at once. Rules never apply to card payments, which have their own logic. Shared rules, made in Multi-Company, are tried after the company's own (see section 9).

            4. How the Engine Decides

            Automatic categorizing

            Configuration > Settings holds each company's switches. All are off unless switched on, except suggestions from past coding:

            SettingEffect
            Append {Engine} to transactions written to QuickBooksMarks every transaction the Engine posts, so it can be told apart in QuickBooks.
            Automatically categorize paymentsFiles card payments from checking and posts them, using the program as vendor and the paying bank account as category.
            Suggestions from past codingFills Vendor and Category when people have coded a merchant the same way at least 5 times with 90% agreement. Never posts. On by default; untick to turn suggestions off for the company.
            Ignore the Dashboard's TeamsOnly this company's own Teams (Broker Data > Teams) name a team in its bank details; the Dashboard's bank details, venues and names are not used for it.
            Exclude from Multi-Company Bank Feed RulesNo Multi-Company rule applies to (or is listed in) this company, even when a rule's companies include it.
            Automatically categorize matchesOff, Strong & Above, Very Strong & Above, or Perfect. Posts a charge's match when it is at that level, clearly ahead of the next candidate, and no other charge wants the same bill as much.
            Automatically categorize STH chargesPosts an STH charge when all four STH checks pass. A charge with a Strong or better bill match is left for the bill instead, so it is never counted twice.

            What fills Vendor and Category

            In order, the first that applies wins: a person's own entry; a Bank Feed Rule; card-payment logic; STH logic; and finally suggestions from past coding.

            Suggestions from past coding

            Only charges a person categorized count. The Engine's own automatic filing never does, so it cannot learn from itself. A suggestion is marked "from history (6 of 6)" and waits for a person to accept it. It is worked out afresh as charges are coded, so one that falls below the agreement needed stops being offered until it is back above it.

            Suggestions on the Bank Feed lists them for this company, one row per bank detail (and whatever else Algorithms > Suggestions matches on), with how many times it was coded alike. Tick Ignore Suggestion to stop one being offered (untick to use it again) and press the row's Save, gray until the tick changes; the bank feed's suggestion badges go or come back with it. The red x deletes it after asking.

            5. QBO Sync

            Bank / Card Accounts

            Every bank and card account in the company's QuickBooks file, with how its transactions arrive. Columns include the account name, type, Bank Feed (API, Plaid or None, with connection details), QBO Balance, Bank Balance, Last Statement Date and Last Statement Balance (credit cards connected through Plaid, for bank reconciliations; hover over a blank to see why, or over a statement to see the two before it, kept as each new one arrives), and when it last synced. Disconnecting a card clears its statement.

            • Rename, change type, retire or restore an account from its row; these change it in QuickBooks too.
            • Connect a card through Plaid from its Bank Feed column. When one bank login has several cards, a window pairs each with its account.
            • Reconnect appears when a bank needs the cardholder to sign in again; it repairs the same connection and keeps its history.
            • Add New Account creates an account in QuickBooks. Sync From QBO refreshes everything from QuickBooks now.
            • Credit card balances show as positive amounts owed.
            • Card types (All Card Types) narrows the grid to Virtuals, Non-Virtuals, API, Plaid or None, with Select All, Clear and Close at the bottom as on the Bank Feed's filters; the grid follows when it closes. Connection Issues Only, below them, keeps only the cards with a red ! (an API feed gone quiet) or a Reconnect (a bank feed that needs signing in again).
            • A Plaid card's CC Last 4/5 comes from the bank's own description when it names the card used ("XXXX3004", "Virtual Account Number 5178"); otherwise it is the connected account's last four.
            • Pages: Bank / Card Accounts, Chart of Accounts and Vendors page like the Bank Feed -- Page size, Prev / Next, the count of results and numbered pages -- over the rows the quick search leaves.

            API Card Connections

            One grid of every connected card program account: program, account, the card account its charges land in, whether its key works, the last pull and its result. Drag a header's edge to resize a column. A pull brings in wires and ACH on the account as well as card spend where the program reports them (Slash and Taekus); Slash's own moves between its accounts and its internal Daily Credit Card Payment sweeps stay out.

            ControlWhat it does
            Add ConnectionIn the grid's last row, at the left. Choose the program (Bill, Global Rewards, Slash or Taekus), then only what it needs: its key (from those added on Configuration > Connectors), Slash's entity and program, Taekus' username, and the card account its charges land in.
            Upload Card ListAbove the grid. Loads the card list spreadsheet Global Rewards provides, each group of cards into the account chosen for it, merged by GR ID.
            Card AccountOn each row, a dropdown: change where that account's charges land.
            Pull From / Pull NowPull one account now, from the date given or from its last pull. While it pulls, the button is a red Stop, which stops after the page it is on and keeps what came in. A Global Rewards account with a long card list pulls in the background.
            StopShown while a long Divvy pull runs; stops it.
            CardsGlobal Rewards rows: the account's card list, edited here when short.
            AccountTaekus rows: the Username it signs in with, on the same line, shown by its first four characters; its red x clears it so a new one can be entered. Slash rows: Change chooses the entity (the company in Slash) and program.
            Red xDisconnects the account; charges already imported stay where they are. It stays listed as Not routed while its key is on Connectors, and its red x then removes it altogether, key included.

            Chart of Accounts and Vendors

            The company's QuickBooks accounts and vendors, mirrored. Rename, change an account's type, and retire or restore entries here. Merge Duplicates folds numbered duplicates such as "Office Supplies (1)" back into their originals when they have no posted transactions; duplicates with history must be merged in QuickBooks.

            Deletion / Edit Scan

            Checks that what the Engine posted is still in QuickBooks as posted. It finds transactions deleted or edited in QuickBooks afterwards, so the Engine and QuickBooks agree. It runs with the hourly refresh; Scan Now runs it immediately.

            6. Broker Data and Configuration (per company)

            Broker Data

            A navigation button of its own, after QBO Sync. Its tabs:

            TabPurpose
            DatabasesWhere the broker keeps its people and its season ticket records: one row for Profiles / Address Book and one for Season Ticket Records, each listing every source it reads, its record count, last sync and a Sync button, which turns into a red Stop while the sync runs. A row's pencil changes its source (below).

            Broker Data sources

            Each source is its own row on Broker Data's Databases tab: Add Source (after the quick search) adds one, for a broker who keeps a table per league or a file per year; a row's pencil changes it and its red x removes it. Removing a part's last source removes every record read from it as well, and nothing is read for that part until a source is added. Each source is Airtable (a base, table and view), Google Sheets or OneDrive (a file and tab, or All Tabs -- One Per Season when each season is its own tab laid out alike, where each tab's name gives its season and one Status column serves every tab; the header row is preselected where it was found, and can be changed). A file the list does not show, such as one in SharePoint or Teams, can be opened by pasting its link and pressing Use Link (OneDrive's Share → Copy link, or the address in the browser while the file is open). OneDrive's file list is re-read when it is over 10 minutes old; Refresh List reads it now. Preview reads a few real rows before anything is saved.

            • Profiles / Address Book: a name column (or first and last name) and an email column, which may hold several addresses. A column linking to another Airtable table is read as the linked records' names.
            • Season Ticket Records: Name (required -- the Name column, or both First and Last Name, so charges can be matched to the holder), Email, Team (required), League, Plan Type, Total Cost and Credit Card, and the status in one of three layouts: one column per season, one column for all seasons, or one row per season (a season column, and a status column or none, when being listed means active). Ignore Rows Where skips rows the source keeps that aren't season tickets, at every sync: as many conditions as needed (Add Condition), any one of which skips a row, each a column, an operator and a value -- for text is, is not, is any of, is none of (comma separated), is empty, is not empty; for amounts = ≠ > < ≥ ≤. Canadian Team Costs Are In says whether Canadian teams' costs are listed in CAD (the expected amount converts them at the charge date's rate) or already in USD (no conversion).
            • Renewal Years (Broker Data's own tab): for each league, how this broker writes the season beginning in 2026 -- the Engine's 26/27 -- chosen from Same, 2026, 2027, 2026/27, 2026-27, 2026/2027, 2026-2027, 2025/26, 2025-26, 2025/2026 or 2025-2026. Choosing 2027 means a single year names the year the season ends; choosing a 2025 one means this broker writes two-year seasons a year behind (2025/26 is the Engine's 26/27). Every league the Dashboard lists is shown, with any this company added itself.
            • Renewal Status Words (Broker Data's own tab): how you notate your season ticket renewal statuses. A grid of Renewal Status, Engine Notation (set on the Dashboard, the same for every company), Your Notation (this company's words, comma separated -- Ticket Guy's 'Yes' beside Renewed, say) and Color (the pill's color on the Bank Feed, set on the Dashboard). Three statuses are built in: Renewed (coded, can be added automatically), In Progress (coded, with the status shown) and Not Renewed (goes to review). A status of the company's own is added on the last row (Add / Clear) with its name, what it is treated as, its words and its color. Each row has its own Save, gray until it changes. A word can mean only one status; a status in none goes to review as unrecognized.
            • Cards named by email address: when a card's name is an email address rather than a person's name, the Engine finds the season ticket records carrying that address (their Email column, or the address book's addresses for the holder), as long as the address belongs to one holder.
            • Teams may be written in full, as a nickname ("Sixers"), a mascot or a short code ("PHI"). The Engine reads them through its directory of NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, MLS, WNBA and major college teams, using the League column where a code or mascot is shared between leagues. The company's own team names win.
            • Seasons follow the schedule, labeled by the year they start: 2026, 2026-2027 and 26/27 are all the 26/27 season (the 2026 MLB season, the NBA season starting in 2026). A source can read a single year as the season ending then instead.
            • The sync reports how many rows it skipped because their season could not be read, and any teams it could not recognize. Every source is read once a day, and whenever it is saved or Sync is pressed.

            What the colors in the Teams grid mean

            Each venue and bank detail in the Teams grid is a claim on bank descriptions. Its look says whether it is used:

            Looks likeWhat it means
            plain textActive: used to identify this team.
            sharedClaims nothing -- another team claims the same text just as strongly, so neither is chosen.
            too shortToo short to identify a team safely (under 8 characters). Saved, but not used; use a longer form.
            outrankedNot used here -- a bank-detail note claims this text, and a note beats a venue.
            offThis team is switched off for matching.

            8. The Audit Log

            Every import, decision, posting, setting change and connection change, with who did it and when. Filter by action, person, date or text, and export to Excel. Inside a company it shows that company; on the Dashboard it shows everything; in Multi-Company it shows every company you can access.

            9. Multi-Company

            Choose Multi-Company in the picker (between the two divider lines) to work across companies in one view. Each grid gains a Company column (after the Action column where there is one), and a Companies picker narrows the view; the choice carries across screens.

            • Every action works within the row's own company: a charge is matched only to its own company's bills and posted to its own company's QuickBooks file.
            • Shared Bank Feed Rules: a rule added here is one shared rule for the companies you tick, edited once for all of them. Its Company cell (Shared · 3 Companies) opens the list of companies using it; unticking a company makes it ignore the rule; the row's Save turns green and applies the change. Multi-Company shows only shared rules. Inside a company, only the shared rules it uses are listed, each with a Shared tag beside its name and an Ignore (Shared Rules) tick -- ticking it takes the rule off that company (it is then used again from Multi-Company). A shared rule can't be removed inside a company -- only in Multi-Company. A company's own rules are tried before shared ones, and a shared rule does not apply where the company's QuickBooks file lacks its vendor or category.
            • Undo reverses your latest action in whichever company it was taken.
            • Configuration is not available in this mode. Merging duplicate accounts needs a single company.

            10. Security

            • API keys are encrypted in the database and never sent to a browser; screens only show them masked.
            • Sign-in requires the two-step code.
            • Profile, in the menu behind your initials, changes your name and your password (which needs your current one).
            • Keep access to the hosting account and the Intuit developer account limited and protected with two-factor sign-in: together they hold everything needed to reach the data.
            • If a key may have been exposed, generate a new one with the provider and paste it on Connectors.

            11. Troubleshooting

            ProblemWhat to do
            "STH is not synced yet" bannerShown on one company's Bank Feed when its Season Ticket Records are set up on Broker Data but none have been read yet. It goes on the next Bank Feed load after the first sync finishes (Sync on Broker Data starts one). It never shows in Multi-Company, on the Dashboard or for a company with no Season Ticket Records source.
            An upload is refusedThe message says what the file needs. A CSV must have a date column and an amount (or debit and credit) column.
            A card stopped syncingLook for Reconnect in its Bank Feed column on Bank / Card Accounts.
            QuickBooks refused a postingThe row stays in For Review with QuickBooks' reason on it. Fix the cause, then post again.
            Transactions appear under the wrong accountCheck the account's name and Type on Bank / Card Accounts.
            A charge was posted by mistakeUse Undo straight away, or Return to For Review from Categorized.
            A match is missingCheck Open Bills for the bill and the Gates in Algorithms; Force Match can pair them by hand.
